# Would OpenEden TBILL pass as collateral in a Morpho market?

**Decision memo.** Author: Marco Amendola. Data as of {{as_of}} (Ethereum block {{state_block}}). Not investment advice.
**Scope:** OpenEden TBILL on Ethereum (`0xdd50C053C096CB04A3e3362E2b622529EC5f2e8a`) against Morpho Blue; the issuer's BNB Chain and Arbitrum deployments are read for supply only. Sources: Morpho API (top 25 V1 and top 25 V2 vaults by TVL, plus a market search by collateral address), public RPC (eth_call and logs), verified contract source, issuer documentation. It describes contract rules and measured behaviour and ranks no curator or issuer.

## 1. Bottom line
**Reject today (supply cap $0). A capped approval is possible only if the issuer's KYC manager lists Morpho Blue and at least three liquidator contracts run by unrelated operators, and only as a pilot the vault accepts losing in full.** The decisive variable is that list: `isKyc(Morpho Blue)` returns **{{state.morpho_blue_isKyc}}**. The issuer can later withdraw the listing, one key posts the NAV that sets the liquidation trigger, and the maintainer can move any balance, so the amount at risk is everything lent, not a share of the token. If listed: LLTV {{derived.limits.lltv|.1f}}% (picked from Morpho's allowed steps, not derived) and a supply cap of ${{derived.limits.cap_m|.1f}}M, a judgment bounded by a 1% vault-exposure rule.

## 2. Thesis and mechanism
The question "is a 3-month T-bill token safe collateral?" is the wrong one. Price risk is small: in the last year NAV moved at most {{nav.max_step_pct_last365}}% up and {{nav.min_step_pct_last365}}% down in one update, and a 100 bp rate shock on under three months' average maturity is about {{derived.exit.rate_shock_100bp_nav_pct}}% (assumption from the issuer's stated maturity). The right question is whether Morpho Blue can **hold, seize and sell** the token, and keep doing so. The transfer hook requires sender and receiver to be KYC-approved and not banned, so liquidation works only while Morpho Blue and the liquidator are both listed. **Falsifier:** if `isKyc` is true for Morpho Blue and three independent liquidators, the first step fails; revocation (G5) is a separate risk that the listing does not remove.

## 5. Case against, and what would change the verdict
**Strongest case against rejecting:** NAV is stable, run-off redemptions settled on normal waits, and the issuer can whitelist protocols (EUTBL and USCC show it done), so "reject" may mean "not yet asked". **Cost of the fix:** a whitelist closes open liquidation, so the {{derived.limits.lif_pct_86}}% incentive is not competed for and the issuer controls who may liquidate. Three unrelated operators is a **judgment call**, and "unrelated" cannot be checked on chain. **Stress case, withdrawals paused during a liquidation:** at the cap a liquidator repaying ${{derived.limits.cap_m|.1f}}M seizes about ${{derived.limits.cap_m|.1f}}M plus a ${{derived.limits.cap_bonus_usd|,.0f}} bonus in tokens it cannot redeem while one key holds `pauseWithdraw`, with no DEX pool. Unpaused waits reached {{queue.latency_h_max}} h; a pause has no bound. The {{derived.limits.nav_margin_to_lltv_pct}}% NAV margin does not cover that: the loss is the exit, not the price.

**Three conditions that change the verdict**
1. **Listing:** Morpho Blue and at least 3 independent liquidator contracts return `isKyc = true` (reject to capped approval).
2. **Redemption liquidity:** an `eth_call` of `redeemIns` at the cap succeeds, or p90 queue latency stays at or under 48 h for 90 days (now {{queue.latency_h_p90}} h). If neither holds the cap halves to ${{derived.limits.cap_half_m|.1f}}M.
3. **Revocation exposure (replaces the maintainer-power condition):** the vault treats the whole cap as a loss it accepts, so exposure to this market is at most 1% of vault assets (a vault of at least ${{derived.limits.vault_min_assets_m|.0f}}M for a ${{derived.limits.cap_m|.1f}}M cap). Anyone can read the triggers that set the cap to $0 for new supply: `isKyc` or `isBanned` changing for Morpho Blue or a listed liquidator, `pausedWithdraw()` true, or the NAV feed older than 5 days. They protect new money, not positions already open. I dropped the 24 h timelock and a written commitment as limits: the signers that would be limited control both.

## 6. Decision rules
- Supply cap ${{derived.limits.cap_m|.1f}}M and at most 1% of vault assets. **Both are judgment, not derived;** the loss they allow is the full amount lent. I dropped a cap of 5% of the Ethereum token's assets: that base moves with bridging (it changed by {{chains.eth_assets_change_pct|.1f}}%, from ${{chains.eth_assets_first_m|.1f}}M on {{chains.two_chain_first_date}} to ${{chains.eth_assets_now_m|.1f}}M now, at today's NAV). The measured anchor: ${{derived.limits.cap_m|.1f}}M is {{derived.limits.cap_vs_median_day|.1f}} times the median settled day. Today it is also {{derived.limits.cap_5pct_share_pct}}% of the Ethereum token's assets.
- LLTV {{derived.limits.lltv|.1f}}%, chosen from Morpho's allowed steps; nothing in the data picks it over 91.5%. Incentive {{derived.limits.lif_pct_86}}% (Morpho formula), against {{derived.limits.lif_pct_915}}% at 91.5% and {{derived.limits.lif_pct_77}}% at 77%. The NAV margin is {{derived.limits.nav_margin_to_lltv_pct}}%.
- Oracle: Chainlink-style adapter on the TBILL feed, which one operator key posts, so the issuer sets the liquidation trigger. Pause the market if the feed is older than 5 days (longest observed gap {{nav.longest_gap_days_last365}} days; the vault reverts at 7).

## 8. Verdict and sizing
**Reject (G1, G2).** If both clear: **approve with limits as a pilot**, supply cap about ${{derived.limits.cap_m|.1f}}M. The size is tied to the downside: in a freeze, burn, revocation or paused exit the loss is the full amount lent, so the cap is what the vault accepts losing outright (at most 1% of its assets), while the {{derived.limits.nav_margin_to_lltv_pct}}% NAV margin covers only price risk. Limits per flag: G3, G5 the full-loss cap and condition 3; G4, G8 the 5-day feed rule; G6, G7 condition 2.
